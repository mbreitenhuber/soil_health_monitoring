User Guide

How to use AgroScan

A step-by-step guide to analyzing your agricultural fields — from drawing your first polygon to reading the soil quality zone map.

Overview

Three steps to your zone map

01
✏️

Draw your field

Use the polygon tool on the map to outline the boundaries of your agricultural field. Click to set each corner until you close the shape.

02

Once your polygon is drawn, choose a date and click Start analysis in the sidebar. The ML model will process your field.

Step-by-Step

Detailed walkthrough

Step 1

Navigate to the Analysis page

Click Analysis in the top navigation bar, or use the Analyze now button on the home page. You will see a full-screen map centered on Germany.

💡 Tip Use the scroll wheel or the + / – buttons on the map to zoom into your region before drawing. This makes it easier to place accurate polygon points.
Step 2

Draw your field polygon

In the top-left corner of the map you will find the drawing toolbar. Select the Polygon tool (the pentagon icon). Then:

  1. Click once on the map to place the first corner of your field.
  2. Continue clicking along the field boundary to add more points.
  3. Close the polygon by clicking on the first point again, or press Finish in the toolbar.

The sidebar will update automatically, showing you the number of vertices and the approximate area of your drawn field.

💡 Tip You do not need to be pixel-perfect. A rough outline of your field is enough for the model to produce a meaningful zone map. You can always edit or delete the polygon and redraw it using the toolbar.
Step 3

Start the analysis

Once a polygon is drawn, you can choose a date at which you want your field to be analysed. The Start analysis button in the sidebar becomes active. Click it to start analysing your field. A loading indicator will appear while the model runs.

⚠️ Note The analysis requires an active internet connection to download the satellite data. If the button remains inactive, make sure your polygon is properly closed on the map.
Step 4

Read your results

After the analysis completes, colored zones will appear on top of your drawn polygon. The sidebar also shows a summary with the number of zones analyzed, the average soil quality score, and the highest and lowest zone values.

To start fresh, click the Reset button to clear all layers and draw a new polygon.

Step 5

Save your results

You can save your results by clicking the Save button in the sidebar. This stores your analysis (including the polygon and summary statistics) so you can view and compare it later in your Personal Dashboard.

Data management

Saving fields & managing your analyses

AgroScan treats a “saved field” as a saved analysis: your drawn polygon plus the selected date and the computed zone statistics. Saving lets you build a history for the same field over time and compare different dates.

💾

What gets saved?

The field polygon (your drawn boundary), the analysis date, summary values (avg/min/max), and the computed zones. This makes it possible to revisit results later without re-running the analysis.

📂

Where do I find it?

Open the Personal Dashboard to see your saved analyses list. Each entry shows the crop (if provided), date, and summary statistics.

🧹

How can I manage saved items?

You can delete saved analyses directly from the dashboard. If you want to re-analyze the same field, simply draw the polygon again (or reuse the same outline) and save a new entry for the new date.

📌 Tip

If you frequently analyze the same field, use saving to create a timeline of results. This is especially useful to track changes after management actions (e.g., irrigation, fertilization, cover crops).

Under the hood

About the ML model & data

🛰️

Sentinel-2

The model is powered by satellite imagery from the European Space Agency's Copernicus programme. Sentinel-2 delivers high-resolution optical imagery including NDVI, NDWI, and other vegetation indices.

📅

Data freshness

Sentinel satellites revisit the same location every 5–6 days. In the worst case, the satellite data used for your analysis may therefore be up to 5–6 days old. For most soil quality assessments this is well within an acceptable margin, but keep this in mind if you recently performed field operations such as irrigation or tilling.

🧠

Model output

The model produces a score between 0.0 and 1.0 for each grid cell inside your polygon. These scores represent relative soil quality based on spectral reflectance, vegetation density, and moisture content indicators derived from the satellite data.

💡 Tip

If you want to learn more about how the ML-Model exactely works and are interested in the research background you can visit the Report page.

FAQ

Common questions

Can I analyze multiple fields in one session?

Currently, one polygon can be active on the map at a time. You can still analyze and save multiple fields by saving the result, clicking Reset, and drawing the next field polygon. Your saved analyses are listed in the Personal Dashboard.

How large can my polygon be?

There is no hard size limit, but very large areas (several hundred hectares) will result in a larger number of grid cells and a slightly longer analysis time. For best results, analyze individual fields or management zones separately.

Why do my results look very similar across runs?

If you analyze the same polygon with the same date, results can be very similar because the same inputs are used. Try selecting a different date, or compare multiple saved runs in the Personal Dashboard to spot changes over time.

Where are saved analyses stored and how do I delete them?

Saved analyses are stored in the app’s database and can be managed from the Personal Dashboard. Use the Delete button on an entry to remove it from your history.

Can I export the results?

Yes, all stored data can be downloaded as CSV-Files. Go to the personal dashboard and use the Download all button or download fields independently.
