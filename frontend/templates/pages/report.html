Introduction

Soil Organic Carbon & Its Importance

Soils can function as an important carbon sink through the process of soil carbon sequestration, where atmospheric CO₂ is stored in the ground as soil organic matter through photosynthesis [1]. When maintained in a healthy state, soils represent the largest terrestrial carbon pool globally [2].

Agricultural soils with saturated soil organic carbon (SOC) levels provide several environmental and agronomic benefits. In addition to binding carbon that would otherwise remain in the atmosphere, high SOC content contributes to improved long-term agricultural productivity, increased biodiversity, and the preservation of ecosystem functions and services [2]. As a result, the enhancement and monitoring of SOC stocks have become increasingly important objectives within environmental and agricultural policy frameworks, including initiatives such as the EU Soil Strategy for 2030 [3].

Soils represent the largest terrestrial carbon pool globally — their health is central to climate, food security, and biodiversity.
Problem Statement

Barriers to Routine SOC Monitoring

Despite the recognized importance of SOC for sustainable soil management (SSM), farmers continue to face substantial bottlenecks when attempting to integrate carbon stock monitoring into their agricultural practices. To reliably assess the impact of sustainable soil management practices, SOC has to be assessed several times over a longer time period.

However, conventional soil sampling approaches are associated with significant cost barriers, labor requirements, and time-intensive field and laboratory procedures, limiting their scalability and routine application across large agricultural areas [4].

Methodology

Machine Learning & Remote Sensing

To address these limitations, machine learning-based remote sensing models have emerged as a promising approach for predicting soil organic carbon content on agricultural fields. By leveraging satellite-derived information and data-driven methods, these models offer the potential for scalable and cost-efficient SOC estimation. A baseline to assess the impact of SSM practices can be established by utilizing historic satellite data.

However, many existing tools and platforms remain restricted by paywalls, limited accessibility, or insufficient methodological transparency, thereby constraining their broader adoption and reproducibility within both research and practical agricultural applications.

Platform

What AgroScan Offers

AgroScan provides a free, open-source platform to support farmers in assessing the soil organic carbon content of their fields, monitoring the effectiveness of SSM practices, and empowers them to advocate in front of policy-makers responsible for soil initiatives.

As an open-source tool it provides both transparency on the underlying methodology and the option to personalize the tool for individual use cases or expand and improve the platform for other users.
